RAQ: a health tracker you’d actually want to wear

Whoop, Oura, and Ultrahuman all track well, but they look like tech. I wanted to see if a tracker could read as jewelry first, for women 18 to 35 who would skip a gadget on their wrist.

Role
Creator & product lead
Timeline
Feb 2026 – present
Built with
Fusion 360, Swift, nRF Connect
01

Scoping the MVP

I started with a teardown of Whoop, Oura, and Ultrahuman, then wrote a product spec that ranked every feature. Only the P0s had to work in version one.

P0 · Must have
  • Resting + continuous heart rate
  • Heart rate during activity
  • Overnight sleep
  • Daily steps
P1 · Should have
  • Heart rate variability
  • Blood oxygen (SpO₂)
  • Calories
P2 · Nice to have
  • Core body temperature
  • Naps
  • No screen, no buttons. A warm amber glow is the only signal on the bracelet. The data lives in the app.
  • Built like jewelry. Gold PVD over nickel-free stainless steel, with a sapphire sensor window on the underside.

03

Fitting the electronics

Everything has to fit in the top bar of the cuff, so I worked from a reference board just 33 × 9 mm. I mapped what lives where, then designed the cuff around it.

  • Set up an embedded dev environment from scratch on Apple Silicon with an nRF54L15 dev kit, a MAX30102 heart-rate sensor, and a BMA456 accelerometer.
04

Designing the form

I used generative AI to help write a Fusion 360 script that builds the whole cuff from a short list of measurements. Change one number, rerun it, and you get a new bracelet. That made it cheap to print variants and test fit and comfort on real wrists.

05

Building the app

First milestone: a bare-bones SwiftUI app that finds my dev board and connects over Bluetooth, with heart rate and steps on screen. Then I vibe-coded the real interface to see how the experience should feel day to day. Health and Profile are still placeholder tabs.
